Privacy

Last changed 15 October 2026

EmberStorm runs on your own computer at home. Your photos, films, music, books, searches, accounts and passwords stay there. Nothing of yours is sent to us, and we have no way to see it.

What we collect

Nothing about you or what you do. EmberStorm and its apps have no accounts with us, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. They do not report what you watch, play, read or search for, to us or to anyone. The one service we run (below) is there to give your server a secure address, and holds nothing about your media.

Where your things are

On the server you set up - your own computer, or an EmberStorm box in your home. Whoever runs that server (usually you, or the person in your household who set it up) is in charge of what is on it. They can add and remove people, and the owner of a server can see what is on it, as the owner of any computer can. Each person's own photos are kept apart: the other members of the household do not see them.

The apps

The iPhone, Apple TV, Android, Google TV and Fire TV apps talk only to the EmberStorm server you choose. They ask for these, and use them only for what is said:

  • Photos (if you turn on photo backup or add media from your phone): your photos and videos are sent to your own server, and nowhere else.
  • Camera: to scan a code, such as the one a TV shows to sign in. The picture is read on the phone and not kept (on Android, by Google Play services' own code scanner).
  • Local network: to find your EmberStorm server at home, so you do not have to type its address.

Signing in keeps a cookie from your server on the device, as a website does. Downloads you choose are kept on the device until you remove them or sign out. While the iPhone and Apple TV apps are in Apple's TestFlight beta, TestFlight passes us crash reports and any feedback you choose to send, as Apple's TestFlight describes; the apps add nothing to it.

What leaves your house

EmberStorm itself contacts the internet only for these:

  • Its secure address. EmberStorm's name service gives your server a ….emberstorm.app address and helps it get a free certificate from Let's Encrypt. The service knows your server's address on your home network, your home's internet address (which any website you visit sees), and the address away from home if you turn on remote access. It keeps a list of which servers are on which internet connection in memory for a few days, so "Open my EmberStorm" on this website can find yours - you can turn that off in Settings. It never carries your media, and it does not know who you are - unless the web address you choose for your server says so (thesmiths.emberstorm.app).
  • Updates. EmberStorm looks at GitHub for a newer release twice a day, and installs it by itself at night - on your own computer unless you turn that off, and on the EmberStorm box. Only releases signed by EmberStorm are installed. The programs come from GitHub and Docker's registries, which see your home's internet address. An EmberStorm box also gets its system's security updates from Debian and sets its clock over the internet.
  • Things you switch on, all off until you do: lyrics found online (LRCLIB), artist bios and similar artists (MusicBrainz, ListenBrainz, Wikipedia), and remote access. Each is sent only what it needs - an artist and song name - and nothing that says who you are. Scrobbling, if you connect your own ListenBrainz account, sends that account what you play, as scrobbling does.
  • Things you do: choosing the right film or show, or a poster for one, asks the film and TV server's online databases (TheMovieDb, the Open Movie Database) by the title, and shows their posters through your server. Checking a show against an episode guide asks TVmaze by the show's name. Importing playlists from Plex signs in to your Plex account, on Plex's own page, and reads your playlists from your Plex server.

The media servers EmberStorm runs for you also go online for their usual jobs: the film and TV server looks up posters and details (from TheMovieDb and similar), the photo server downloads its search models once and checks for its own new versions, speech recognition for making ebooks downloads its model once, and the read-along server downloads its speech model once and checks its changelog. On Windows and Mac, EmberStorm runs inside Docker Desktop, which has its own privacy policy and sends Docker usage statistics unless you turn them off in its settings.

This website

emberstorm.app is hosted by GitHub Pages, which keeps the usual server logs (GitHub's privacy statement covers them). The site sets no cookies and has no analytics. "Open my EmberStorm" asks our name service which servers are on your internet connection. The name service runs on Railway, which keeps ordinary short-lived logs of requests (an internet address and a time); we keep nothing more.

Children

EmberStorm collects nothing from anyone, children included. The owner of a server decides which shelves each person in the household can see.
